01 · Who is asking

No lookup. No vendor. No hop.

The session cookie is set by your server, signed with your key and checked in memory. In our benchmark of the reference code that took 1.79 microseconds at the median.{{c:exp-verify}} If Core is down, this still works.

One first-party cookie. No device fingerprint, in any mode.

02 · What it costs

Paths have prices, not reputations.

Static files are free. A page is 1. Search is 5, a login attempt 10, checkout 20. Those are the proposed defaults; the price list is a file you own, and your app can report the real cost of an expensive response.

search: { match: /api/search*, cost: 5, min_tier: 1 }

03 · Who pays

The session pays. The refill forgives.

Every session regains 20 units a minute, up to 300, and three times faster once someone signs in. Reading, comparing, checking out: ordinary use spends slower than it refills.

212 − 5 = 207. That is the whole calculation.

04 · What you decided

Your rules, in your order.

A policy file says what happens when a balance can't pay, when a network mints sessions in a burst, or when the five-minute loop raises a flag. Nothing acts that you didn't write down.

over-budget → next challenge · else throttle

05 · The answer

Allow, challenge or throttle. Before your code runs.

The decision lands on the request before your handler runs. Anything other than allow is written to your decision log with a reference the visitor can quote to support.

req.br = { action: "allow", credits: 207 }

Act II Pay

Out of credit? Pay in work. The next pass buys half as much.

A challenge here isn't a gate you pass once. It's a purchase. It buys credit, never clearance, and each repeat buys less.

TIER 1 · INVISIBLE

Proof-of-work

The browser does a few small sums in the background. Plain HTTP clients can't.

THE FLOOR

Wait

When the grants are spent: 429 and Retry-After. The refill still flows, slowly.

refill only

A resold pass stops being worth reselling.

One challenge per session with no rate limit behind it adds latency, not limits: throughput doesn't fall.{{c:anubis-throughput}} Here, the first human check buys 3,000 units, the second 1,500, then 750, 375 and 188. Then there is nothing left to buy, and the session waits on the refill.

Act IV Own

It lives in your app. The receipts live in your bucket.

Most bot defense is a service your traffic passes through. BotRampart is software you run, and the data never leaves.

No hop added

The SDK is a filter in your framework. Your CDN keeps handling floods, as it does today.

Keeps working alone

Cookies are verified offline. If Core is unreachable, tier 1, budgets and your rules carry on with the last policy.

The model can't block anyone

Every five minutes a decision model reads named bands, never what a visitor typed. It can raise a price. It can't block or allow a client.

Straight answer

If Cloudflare already works for you, keep it.

BotRampart is for teams who can't, or won't, route their traffic through a vendor. Everyone else may already have something good, and often free. The field names who to use, and when.

Built for

  • Engineering-led shops, marketplaces, SaaS, travel and ticketing sites with roughly 5 to 200 million requests a month, scrapers or fake sign-ups, and no security team.
  • Teams priced out of enterprise bot management, whose entry plans start in the thousands a month.{{c:dd-entry-price}}
  • Regulated and data-sovereign teams: fintech, health, government suppliers, EU residency.
  • Apps on private networks or behind several CDNs.

Not for you, yet

  • You're happy behind Cloudflare. Its Turnstile challenge is free, with unlimited challenges.{{c:cf-turnstile-free}}
  • You need DDoS protection. Floods belong at the edge; BotRampart refuses requests inside your app.
  • You need mobile apps or partner APIs covered now. The first build is browsers only.
  • You need it in production this quarter. It's in design.
