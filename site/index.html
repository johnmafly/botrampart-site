Open-source bot defense, self-hosted

Price the request, not the visitor.

BotRampart never decides who is a bot. It runs inside your own app server, gives every session a budget and every path a cost, and asks heavy users to pay with work: a proof-of-work, then a human check, then a wait. No proxy in front of your site, no device fingerprint, and the record of every decision stays in your own bucket.

01 · Why now

Telling bots from people has become a guess you can buy your way past.

More than half of web traffic is automated, the puzzles meant to separate people from machines are solved by machines, and the IP addresses that used to identify an abuser now rent by the gigabyte. A verdict of "bot" or "human" is cheap to fake. A budget still has to be spent.

53%

of the web traffic Thales observed in 2025 was automated, by its annual count.{{c:thales-automated}}

100%

of reCAPTCHA v2 image challenges were solved by a published model in a 2024 study.{{c:recaptcha-solved}}

$1–$2.99

per 1,000 solved reCAPTCHA v2 challenges at a solver service; people and models do the clicking for you.{{c:solver-prices}}

65.3%

of 21,491 domains in DataDome's 2026 test stopped none of its 10 test bots. A vendor's test, and it favours the vendor's case.{{c:dd-unprotected}}

"Is this a bot?" A classifier decides, and you defend the answer.

Every false positive is a customer you blocked and cannot explain. Every pass is a clearance that can be resold. Solving the challenge once buys the session unlimited throughput.

"What has this session spent?" Arithmetic decides, and anyone can check it.

Ordinary browsing stays under the refill rate and never sees a challenge after the first invisible one. Heavy use runs out of credit, and each challenge buys a bounded, shrinking amount more. Nobody is labelled; everybody is metered.

02 · How it works

Three loops at three speeds, and only the fastest one is in the request path.

The backend SDK decides each request in memory, with no network call. A five-minute loop looks for patterns no single session shows. People stay in charge of the policy.

Microseconds · in your app

Per request

The backend SDK checks the signed session cookie, charges the path's cost to the session's budget and runs your policy rules. Allow, challenge, throttle or block, before your handler runs.

Every 5 minutes · in Core

Per window

Core turns the last window into named bands ("cost x6 over baseline", "new sessions over 60%") and asks a decision model typed questions. The answer becomes fields your rules can use. It can raise a price; it can never block.

03 · Control

It runs where your app runs. Your traffic data never leaves.

Most bot defense is a service your traffic passes through: a proxy, an edge network, or a module that asks the vendor about every request. BotRampart is software you run.

  • No proxy in the request path. The SDK is a framework filter in your app. Your CDN or WAF keeps handling floods, as it does today.
  • Verified offline. The session cookie is signed with your key and checked in memory. If Core is unreachable, tier 1, budgets and rules keep working.
  • Your bucket, open formats. Rollups, decisions and every model answer are Parquet and JSON in your R2, S3 or MinIO, readable with DuckDB without asking us.
  • No fingerprint ID. One server-set session cookie, an optional hashed login ID, and IP only as a network bucket. No canvas, font or audio fingerprinting in any mode.
  • Open source. SDKs under Apache-2.0; Core and the agent under AGPL-3.0 with a commercial licence for those who need one.

05 · Who it is for

Built for teams who cannot, or will not, route their traffic through a vendor.

And honest about who should use something else. If a CDN in front of your site is fine with you, the incumbents already do a lot for free.

A good fit

  • Engineering-led SaaS, shops, marketplaces, travel and ticketing sites with roughly 5 to 200 million requests a month, scraping, fake sign-ups or credential stuffing, and no security team.
  • Teams priced out of enterprise bot management, whose entry plans start in the thousands of dollars a month.{{c:dd-entry-price}}
  • Regulated and data-sovereign teams: fintech, health, government suppliers, EU residency rules.
  • Apps on private networks or behind several CDNs, which cannot sit behind one vendor's edge.

Probably not for you, yet

  • You already run behind Cloudflare and are happy: its free plan includes Bot Fight Mode and Turnstile is free with unlimited challenges.{{c:cf-turnstile-free}}
  • You need volumetric DDoS protection. BotRampart refuses requests in your app; floods belong at the edge.
  • You need mobile apps or partner APIs protected now. The first build covers browsers only.
  • You need something in production this quarter. BotRampart is in design; the comparison names what to use today.
