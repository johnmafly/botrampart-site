Cloud

You pay for sessions that pass the proof-of-work. Never for blocked traffic.

Cloud bills monthly: a plan fee covers an allowance of protected sessions, and sessions above it are billed per 1,000.

A protected session is one that passed the invisible proof-of-work this month, counted once however many requests it makes. Traffic that never passes is never billed, so a flood of blocked requests can't inflate your invoice. A headless browser that solves the proof-of-work does count as a session; its budget, not your bill, is what holds it back.

Free pauses at the cap, and your site keeps running on the SDK's last policy. A cohort is a group of sessions the decision model is asked about when it drifts from its own baseline (see The meter); Free asks only about the site as a whole. Each plan's limit keeps its model bill below its fee.

Guard-rails on the invoice

  • Never billed: blocked, failed and unchallenged traffic. ALTCHA's cloud makes the same promise for blocked requests.{{c:altcha-sentinel}}
  • A hard cap you set, above which Cloud stops counting instead of charging.
  • Attack waiver. Sessions during a declared attack aren't billed. Fingerprint doesn't bill API calls made while a site is under attack.{{c:fp-attack}}
  • Recomputable. The count is a shipped query over your own decision log. Check the invoice without asking us.

Why not charge per bot stopped?

Because BotRampart doesn't decide who is a bot, and a vendor that detects, judges and bills its own unit has every reason to count high. Per-event pricing also turns an attack into a bill: AWS WAF bills Targeted Bot Control at $10 per million requests and charges for a challenge whether or not the visitor attempts it.{{c:aws-bot-control}}

For scale: reCAPTCHA charges $1 per 1,000 assessments above 100,000 a month,{{c:recaptcha-bill}} hCaptcha Pro is $99 to $139 a month{{c:hcaptcha-pro}} and DataDome's entry plan lists at $3,830 a month.{{c:dd-entry-price}} They bill different units, so these place BotRampart's prices in the market rather than compare like with like.
