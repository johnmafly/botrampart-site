Pricing · proposal

Free to run yourself. Pay for convenience, never for the core.

The whole product is open source and free to self-host. Paid editions sell what a company needs around it: no BotRampart mark, sign-on and audit, support, or someone else running Core. Every price on this page is a proposal to test; nothing is for sale yet.

Cloud

A fixed fee plus protected sessions.

A protected session is one that passed the invisible proof-of-work in the month, counted once however many requests it makes. Bots that never pass are never billed, so an attack cannot inflate your invoice and a false positive is never counted as a success.

Guard-rails on the invoice

  • Never billed: blocked, failed and unchallenged traffic. ALTCHA's cloud makes the same promise for blocked requests.{{c:altcha-sentinel}}
  • Hard cap you set, above which Cloud stops counting instead of charging.
  • Attack waiver. Sessions during a declared attack are not billed. Fingerprint does not bill API calls made while a site is under attack.{{c:fp-attack}}
  • Recomputable. The count is a shipped query over your own decision log, so you can check the invoice without asking us.

Why not charge per bot stopped?

Because BotRampart does not decide who is a bot, and because a vendor that detects, judges and bills its own unit has every reason to count high. Per-event pricing also turns an attack into a bill: AWS WAF bills Targeted Bot Control at $10 per million requests and charges for a challenge whether or not the visitor attempts it.{{c:aws-bot-control}} "Requests mitigated" will appear on your invoice as a number worth knowing, not as the price.

For scale: reCAPTCHA charges $1 per 1,000 assessments above 100,000 a month,{{c:recaptcha-bill}} hCaptcha Pro is $99 to $139 a month,{{c:hcaptcha-pro}} IntelliFend lists $749 a month for 20 million requests{{c:if-price}} and DataDome's entry plan $3,830 a month.{{c:dd-entry-price}} These products bill different units, so the figures place BotRampart's proposal in the market rather than compare like with like.

Promises

The open core stays whole.

  • Nothing shipped is taken back. A feature that reaches the Community edition stays there.
  • The Community edition builds with the commercial directory deleted, and the test suite checks it.
  • The mark is a trademark, not a licence trap. You may fork and remove it; you may not ship the fork under the BotRampart name. The paid build is the convenient, supported way to remove it.
  • No telemetry required for billing. Self-hosted editions are priced per organisation and site, with an offline licence file.
